[6] In Heikkila McDonald JA for the court said this: Rule 244.1 (Drop Dead Rule) [23] As indicated previously, the chambers judge simply adopted the master’s ruling with respect to the Rule 244.1 application wherein the master had concluded: [37] This application was brought in 2008, four years after the filing of the “comprehensive affidavit of records” with the result that there is no issue about that affidavit being filed in an attempt to thwart such an application or as part of a “foot race to the court house”. [38] Therefore, the application under rule 244.1, is denied as the filing of the comprehensive affidavit of records was a thing that materially advanced the action and it occurred within the alleged five year gap. [24] Neither the master nor the chambers judge dealt with the issue as to whether the last thing done “that materially advances the action” must advance it against each defendant separately or whether it simply needs to advance the action as a whole. [25] There are two lines of authorities on this issue. The first line of authority requires that the action must be materially advanced in relation to each defendant: see, for example, Aircare Respiratory Homecare Services Ltd v Alberta (Treasury Branches) (2003), 350 AR 43 (QB); Continental Earthmovers Ltd v Midwest Furnishing & Supplies Ltd, 2006 ABQB 543 (CanLII), 403 AR 180; Danek v Calgary (City of), 2006 ABQB 807 (CanLII). The second line of authority holds that a thing done in an action materially advancing it as a whole is sufficient to avoid dismissal for delay: see, for example, Protasiwich v Archer Memorial Hospital, 1999 ABQB 595 (CanLII), 251 AR 332; Crowfoot Recreational )IILnaC( 341 BQBA 5102 Page: 3 Association v Mackin, 1999 ABQB 299 (CanLII), 268 AR 391; 393008 Alberta Ltd v Basiuk, 2004 ABQB 239 (CanLII), [2004] AJ No 360 [26] Later on in his judgment (at paragraphs 11 and 12) the chambers judge referred to authorities dealing with whether the thing must materially advance the action in relation to each defendant or simply need it materially advance the action as a whole. This however was in the context of his reasons relating to the Rule 244 application. [27] Given the facts of this case, the issue as to whether or not a thing must advance the action as a whole or must advance it against each defendant did not arise. It is clear from the facts that the filing of the Comprehensive Affidavit of Records on July 24, 2004 was a thing that advanced the action against the sole remaining defendant, that being Apex. I see no error on the part of the chambers judge. [28] Although it was not necessary in deciding the outcome of this part of the appeal, I feel that this court should clarify and determine which of the two conflicting lines of authority apply in interpreting Rule 244.1. [29] Rule 244.1(1) reads: 244.1(1) Subject to Rule 244.2, where 5 or more years have expired from the time that the last thing was done in an action that materially advances the action, the Court shall, on the motion of a party to the action, dismiss that portion or part of the action that relates to the party bringing the motion. [30] As mentioned previously in paragraph 25, there are two conflicting lines of authority dealing with the interpretation of Rule 244.1. For example, in Continental Earthmovers Ltd. v Midwest Furnishing & Supplies Ltd. (c.o.b. Midwest Developments) it was stated at para 19: The Plaintiff argues that any thing that advances the action advances it against all parties. But the Rule contemplates things that advance the action against one defendant, but not another: Aircare Respiratory Homecare Services Ltd., v. Alberta (Treasury Branch) (2003), 50 Alta. L.R. (4th) 81, 350 A.R. 43, 44 C.P.C. (5th) 144. Rule 244.1 states that when nothing is done to materially advance an action, the Court shall on the motion of a party to the action, dismiss that portion or part of the action that “relates to the party bringing the motion.” The Plaintiff having done nothing to advance the action against Sovereign General for five years, the action against Sovereign General must be dismissed pursuant to Rule 244.1. [31] On the other hand, in Crowfoot Recreational Association the court ruled at para 9: In my view rule 244.1 cannot read as the BLK group argue. The rule does not state that the things done in the action must materially advance the action against each individual defendant. )IILnaC( 341 BQBA 5102 Page: 4 The thing must only materially advance the action. Since the other defendants have filed statements of defence in 1996 and this materially advanced the action five or more years have not yet expired to require the action be dismissed pursuant to rule 244.1. [32] In my view, the proper interpretation of Rule 244.1 is represented by the second line of authority, namely that the thing need only materially advance the action as a whole. [33] With due respect to the position adopted in Continental Earthmovers Ltd. v Midwest Furnishings & Supplies Ltd. (c.o.b. Midwest Developments), it is better understood as reflecting the proposition that, generally, the Rules do not provide for a party defendant to apply for relief on behalf of other defendants. Accordingly, the words in the second portion of Rule 244.1 make it clear that if a party applies, it only applies on its own behalf. [34] The wording does not mean that the thing must materially advance the action against the individual defendant bringing the Rule 244.1 application. Rule 244.1 describes the thing only as “the last thing [that] was done that materially advances the action”, not “the last thing [that] was done that materially advances the action against the party bringing the application to dismiss”. [35] I am buttressed in this view by the proposition that if there is more than one plausible interpretation of a rule, it is generally to be reconciled in a manner most favourable to the preservation of the right to litigate: Filipchuk v Ladouceur, 2001 ABCA 26 (CanLII), 277 AR192. [36] That this interpretation is the correct one can be illustrated by way of the following example. An action has been commenced against several defendants. The plaintiff’s claim against one of those defendants (the first defendant) is in a position to proceed to trial very early on in the proceedings. However, owing to a variety of factors, it takes much longer to be in a position to enter the matter for trial against the remaining defendants. The first defendant was properly named, and this is not an appropriate case for severance. [37] Eventually, the action is ready to be entered for trial. However before the plaintiff does so, the first defendant applies for an order pursuant to Rule 244.1 since five years and two weeks have elapsed since the last thing was done in the action that materially advanced the action as against the first defendant. [38] On the interpretation favoured by cases such as Continental Earthmovers, the application would have to be granted. This is not a proper or just result. However, a party in the position of the first defendant is not without remedy. That defendant can apply to court for case management with a view to having the whole action proceed in a more expeditious fashion, if that be its desire. This follows from R v Arcand, 2010 ABCA 363 (CanLII) at paras 209-2011, which say: D. Reserved Decisions and Circulation [209] For many years, this Court has required circulation of draft written decisions containing new law to all members of the Court for their comment. A number of other courts of appeal in Canada also follow this procedure. Making new law is important, and so deserves more care and checking; comments from judges off the panel are a very valuable safety net. All judges who wish to comment on the principles of law are given a chance to suggest improvements, clarification, or criticisms. Circulation of a draft is not a plebiscite but rather a chance to criticize, improve, and debate principles of law. The Chief Justice may order the appeal reargued with a larger panel. That has happened from time to time.
